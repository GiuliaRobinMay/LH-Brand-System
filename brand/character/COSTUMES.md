# Matthew — the wardrobe

Three approved costumes. Each is a **signal**, not decoration: the right Matthew
carries the right message before a word is read.

Use with `GENERATION.md`, which holds the prompt template and the rules.

---

## The three costumes 🔒

### 1. Yellow — Welcome & everyday

The signature. Default Matthew. Community welcome, everyday posts, anything with no
more specific job.

> **COSTUME:** a bright yellow single-breasted suit jacket and matching yellow trousers
> printed all over with bold black question marks in varying sizes and rotations, a black
> shirt underneath printed with large flat multicoloured polka dots in red, blue, green,
> yellow and magenta, a yellow belt with a gold buckle.

### 2. Blue — Teach & explain

Roadmap steps, Call Sheet Classes, Application Classes, guides, how-to.

> **COSTUME:** a rich royal blue single-breasted suit jacket and matching royal blue
> trousers, printed all over with bold WHITE question marks in varying sizes and rotations.
> Underneath, a black shirt printed with large flat multicoloured HEARTS in red, blue,
> yellow, cyan and green, each heart outlined in bold black. A coloured belt with a gold
> buckle, black shoes.

Hearts under the teaching suit are deliberate: teaching *is* the caring act in this
brand. He doesn't lecture, he helps.

### 3. Green — Celebrate

Success Stories, wins, approvals, "it came through".

> **COSTUME:** a bright grass-green single-breasted suit jacket and matching green
> trousers, printed all over with bold YELLOW question marks in varying sizes and
> rotations. Underneath, a black shirt printed with large flat multicoloured polka dots in
> red, blue, green, yellow and magenta. A coloured belt with a gold buckle, black shoes.

## Wardrobe rules 🔒

Learned the hard way. Each one comes from a rejected image.

**1. The motif is always question marks.** Never blocks, confetti, abstract shapes or
any other device. The print varies by **colour and ground** — never by shape. The
question mark is what makes any suit unmistakably his.

**2. The shirt ground is always black**, with large flat shapes in brand colours and
bold black outlines. Never a plain white shirt — it reads corporate instantly.

**3. The shirt is never plainer than the suit.** He does not do quiet underneath. A
plain black shirt and a necktie were both rejected for this reason.

**4. Nothing corporate below the neck.** No neckties, no bow ties, no plain black belt.
Belts are coloured, with a gold buckle.

**5. One print per costume, and prints don't repeat across adjacent jobs.** Dots on
yellow and green, hearts on blue. The print is part of how a costume is recognised.

---

## Not adopted, and why

**Orange** — he wears it in real photographs, but orange sits between yellow
(attention) and red (danger), the two most safety-critical signals the brand owns. It
would blur exactly the distinction that must never blur. Real Matthew can wear it;
illustrated Matthew doesn't.

**The multicoloured confetti suit** — a real garment and a good one, but its print is an
intricate irregular photographic pattern that flat comic style cannot reconstruct from a
written description. Repeated attempts produced something that read as a white suit with
scattered marks. If it is ever wanted, the only route is feeding a **photograph of the
actual suit** as a second reference image alongside the character reference. Do not try
to describe it.

**A dedicated Protect costume** — attempted in dark navy and in red; both rejected. The
dark version also dropped his laugh, which made him read as cold. Open question, and
probably the wrong question: a warning may be better served by yellow Matthew beside a
red warning graphic than by dressing him for bad news. Decide deliberately before
generating again.
