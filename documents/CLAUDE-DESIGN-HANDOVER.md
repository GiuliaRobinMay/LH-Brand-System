# Lesko Help — brand handover

Paste this whole document into Claude Design as the brief. It is self-contained:
every value, rule and reference it needs is written out here.

---

## Read this first

You are designing for **Lesko Help** — a membership community that helps everyday
Americans find and apply for government grants, benefits and free programs.

**The audience is the most important constraint.** Members are adults, skewing older,
often financially desperate, frequently disabled or caring for someone who is, with mixed
reading and tech ability. Many are on old phones. Real posts, unedited: *"How do I get
money."* · *"I'm so confused on how to navigate."* · *"I need help finding any grant I can
apply for before I give up."*

Design that photographs well but fails this audience is a failure. Thin type, low-contrast
greys, dense layouts and clever minimalism are all wrong here.

**Status marking.** Sections below are marked **SETTLED** or **OPEN**. Settled means use it
without asking. Open means it is a proposal — do not treat it as decided, and do not invent
a value to fill the gap. Ask.

---

## 1. The story — SETTLED

**The villain, and there are two.**

*The hidden door* — money that is legally yours, sitting behind forms, jargon and the
belief that it isn't for people like you. The founder's own words: *"I got bored of helping
fat cats after a while and thought, why doesn't everyone know this information?"*

*Its parasite* — people who charge for what is free, including impersonators posing as the
founder to rob the members he is trying to help. Not hypothetical: community members have
lost real money to a fake "grant administrator" and then been asked for thousands more.

**What is at stake.** The house on Friday. The car that keeps the job. The procedure
Medicare won't cover.

**The main character.** Someone told no so many times they have started saying it to
themselves. The word they use about themselves is *desperate*. The word they fear is
*stupid*.

**The guide.** Matthew Lesko. Eighty-two. Eccentric on purpose. Members call him **Mr.
Question Man** — a name they gave him. The only figure in the category who never asks for
more money.

**The practice.** Asking. He doesn't write your grant — he teaches you to ask, which is
what the question mark on his suit has meant all along. The costume is the thesis, not a
gimmick.

**The promise.** *Don't give up. Somebody will answer.*

**The single strongest sentence the brand owns:**

> Your subscription is all the money he will ever ask for.

---

## 2. Voice — SETTLED

These phrases grew in the community. Members repeat them to each other unprompted. Use
them; do not invent replacements.

- *"Don't give up."* / *"Don't give up. Ever."*
- *"Stay safe out there."*
- *"It was right on time."*

**How to write:** short sentences, one idea per line, plain words, no wind-up. Write at the
level members actually write.

**Never:** hype · urgency countdowns · the word "secrets" · anything implying government
affiliation · guaranteed outcomes or amounts · invented dollar figures (real and sourced,
or bracketed as `[AMOUNT]`).

**The emotional truth:** a win is not about the size of the cheque. It is that *somebody
finally answered*. Design should feel like a hand raised, never a sales page.

## 3. Vocabulary — SETTLED

**The Grant Roadmap, seven steps, in this order:**

1. Daily Welcome Tour
2. Call Sheet Classes
3. AI Grant Researcher
4. Questions Channel

This is the spine of the member journey. It should look identical everywhere it appears so

**Proper nouns:** Navigators (member guides who run live sessions) · LeskoHelpers (what

## 4. Colour — VALUES SETTLED, PAIRINGS OPEN

### The eight brand colours

| Tier | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Blue | `#3655b3` |
| Primary | Yellow | `#fdcc0a` |
| Support | Green | `#2ca01c` |
| Support | Mid blue | `#0060ff` |
| Support | Red | `#f93946` |
| Accent | Purple | `#7c3bb0` |
| Accent | Pink | `#ff24b1` |
| Accent | Teal | `#00d6f6` |

Neutrals in use: ink `#0e1a2b` · grey `#4a5666` · surface `#f4f6f8` · white `#ffffff`.

### Measured contrast against white

| Colour | Ratio | Can it carry body text on white? |
|---|---|---|
| Blue `#3655b3` | 6.76 : 1 | Passes 4.5, fails 7 |
| Purple `#7c3bb0` | 6.75 : 1 | Passes 4.5, fails 7 |
| Mid blue `#0060ff` | 5.10 : 1 | Passes 4.5, fails 7 |
| Red `#f93946` | 3.68 : 1 | No |
| Pink `#ff24b1` | 3.42 : 1 | No |
| Green `#2ca01c` | 3.41 : 1 | No |
| Teal `#00d6f6` | 1.76 : 1 | No |
| Yellow `#fdcc0a` | 1.52 : 1 | No |

This is not a fault. These colours are built to be seen, which is right for this brand.

### The one pairing that is certain

**Ink `#0e1a2b` on yellow `#fdcc0a` — 11.5 : 1.** The strongest pairing the palette
contains, already in use. It is why yellow is a *surface* colour, never a text colour.

### ⚠️ OPEN — do not decide this yourself

How the other colours pair is **not settled**. An earlier draft invented darkened values
(a deep red for warnings) that are not part of this palette; those are withdrawn. Three
options are on the table and the client is choosing:

- **A** — never text on colour. The eight are fills, shapes and grounds only; all text is
  ink on white or ink on yellow.
- **B** — add a text-safe darkened sibling of each colour.
- **C** — keep 7:1 for body copy but allow 4.5:1 for large text, which lets blue, purple
  and mid blue carry headlines.

**Until this is decided: use the eight colours as fills and grounds, and set all text in
ink on white or ink on yellow.** That is safe under all three options.

## 5. Typography — PROPOSED, not signed off

Two families, both free on Google Fonts.

- **Anton** — display. Used for **H1 only**, once per surface. The moment it appears twice
  on a page it stops being a shout and becomes shouting.
- **DM Sans** — everything else.

| Level | Font | Size | Weight / line-height |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Anton | 48–64px | 1.0, tight tracking, uppercase |
| H2 | DM Sans | 32px | 800 / 1.15 |
| H3 | DM Sans | 24px | 700 / 1.25 |
| Lead | DM Sans | 21px | 400 / 1.5 |
| Body | DM Sans | **18px floor** | 400 / 1.6 |
| Quote | DM Sans | 24–28px | 500 italic / 1.35–1.4 |
| Meta | DM Sans | 16px, never lower | 500 / 1.4 |
| Button | DM Sans | 18px | 700 |

## 6. Accessibility floor — SETTLED, outranks aesthetics

Raised above the usual defaults because of who the audience is.

- Body text **18px** minimum on screen, **12pt** minimum in print
- Contrast **7:1** for body text where achievable, never below 4.5:1
- Hit targets **48px** minimum
- Line length 45–75 characters
- **Never carry meaning by colour alone** — always pair with a word or an icon
- Assume a phone, an old screen, and a reader who is stressed

## 7. The character — SETTLED

Matthew is drawn, not photographed. The illustrated version is the brand's most valuable
asset because it is the one thing an impersonator cannot copy.

### Three forms, three jobs

| Form | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Illustrated Matthew** | The guide inside the product — roadmap steps, guides, thumbnails, teaching, wayfinding | Ownable, reproducible, accessible, impossible to counterfeit |
| **Real photo and video** | Proof of a living person — live sessions, testimonials | Authenticity and warmth |
| **AI-generated photoreal** | **Never** | It is exactly what the impersonators produce |

That last row is a hard rule, not a preference.

### What he looks like

Long expressive face, high domed forehead, receding hairline of bright white hair swept
upward into wild pointed spikes. Warm light skin, deep friendly laugh lines, prominent
cheekbones, strong chin, large ears. Large bright blue eyes crinkled at the outer corners
with genuine delight, thin white eyebrows arched high, behind **oversized eyeglasses with
thick tangerine-orange rims and turquoise-blue temple arms**. A wide open joyful laugh
that lifts his cheeks — playful and mischievous, like a man about to share a wonderful
secret.

**Build:** tall, lean and lanky — roughly eight heads tall, legs slightly more than half
his height, small head relative to the body, narrow shoulders. Never short, stocky,
big-headed or stubby-legged.

**Rendering:** clean 2D vector comic-book art. Bold black outlines of even weight, flat
cel-shaded colour with minimal soft shading and small white highlights, bright saturated
palette. No gradients, no photographic texture, no 3D. Cut out on white, occasional soft
grey contact shadow.

**Signature effect:** exactly three small amber-orange triangular spark marks near a
raised hand or the head.

### If you generate him — the rules that matter

1. **Never generate from text alone.** Always attach one of the reference stills above.
   The originals were made from real photographs and brought in as files; a generation
   with an empty reference slot invents a different man every time.
2. **Never ban his features.** Banning spiky hair, bared teeth, wide eyes or sharp
   features deletes the character — those *are* him. Keep the avoid-list to one line:
   text, watermarks, logos, extra people, photorealism, 3D. Nothing about his face or hair.
3. **Never write his real name in a prompt.** He is a real public figure; naming him makes
   image models refuse or quietly degrade. Describe by attributes only.
4. **Full-body poses need a 2:3 portrait frame.** A wide pose in a square frame forces the
   figure to scale down and produces short legs regardless of what the prompt says about
   height. Square is only for chest-up crops.

**The rule that fixes the unsettling face:** *wild hair and a big open laugh are not the
problem — unsmiling eyes are.* A wide-open mouth with flat staring eyes reads as menacing;
the same mouth with crinkled eye corners, lifted cheeks and raised brows reads as charming.

## 8. The wardrobe — SETTLED

Each costume is a signal, not decoration, so the right Matthew carries the right message
before a word is read.

| Costume | Shirt | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow** suit, black question marks, yellow belt with gold buckle | Black shirt, large flat multicoloured polka dots (red, blue, green, yellow, magenta) | Welcome & everyday. The signature. |
| **Royal blue** suit, white question marks | Black shirt, large flat multicoloured **hearts** (red, blue, yellow, cyan, green) | Teach & explain — roadmap, classes, guides |
| **Grass green** suit, yellow question marks | Black shirt, multicoloured polka dots | Celebrate — Success Stories, wins |

Hearts under the teaching suit are deliberate: teaching is the caring act in this brand.

### Wardrobe rules — each learned from a rejected image

1. **The motif is always question marks.** Never blocks, confetti or abstract shapes. The
   print varies by colour and ground, never by shape.
2. **The shirt ground is always black**, with large flat shapes in brand colours and bold
   black outlines. Never a plain white shirt — it reads corporate instantly.
3. **The shirt is never plainer than the suit.** He does not do quiet underneath.
4. **Nothing corporate below the neck.** No neckties, no bow ties, no plain black belt.
   Belts are coloured.
5. **One print per costume.**

### Tried and rejected — don't repeat these

- **Orange** — he wears it in real life, but orange sits between yellow (attention) and
  red (danger), the two most safety-critical signals the brand owns.
- **The multicoloured confetti suit** — a real garment, but its print is an intricate
  irregular photographic pattern that flat comic style cannot reconstruct from words. It
  would need a photograph of the actual suit as a second reference image.
- **A dedicated warning costume** — attempted in dark navy and in red, both rejected. The
  dark version also dropped his laugh and read as cold.

### The yellow-suit tension, resolved

Yellow is both his signature suit and the "pay attention" colour. **The yellow suit is
identity, not signal.** The attention role belongs to yellow *surfaces* — highlighter bars,
callout blocks, ink on yellow. Suit and surface do different jobs.

## 9. Other brand devices

- **The question mark** is the core mark — asking is the practice. **SETTLED** as a brand
  device. **OPEN:** how far it extends. An earlier draft used it for space logos, badges,
  callout markers, banner pattern and the wordmark all at once, which was too much of one
  device. Needs a deliberate limit.
- **The heart from the playing-card suits** is a real and important brand device and
  **stays**. It belongs to the vocabulary alongside the question mark.
- **A `LESKO?HELP` wordmark** (question mark as connector, in yellow) exists in the current
  Quick Guide masthead but has **never been confirmed** as the lockup. **OPEN.**

## 11. Anti-patterns — never do these

- AI-generated photoreal Matthew (see section 7)
- Anything that reads scammy: urgency timers, "secrets", fake official seals, implied
  government affiliation, guaranteed amounts
- Invented dollar figures
- Small, low-contrast, dense layouts — they fail this audience specifically
- Corporate calm applied to Matthew himself. The frame gets calm so *he* can stay loud,
  never the reverse.
